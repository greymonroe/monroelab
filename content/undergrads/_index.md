        The Monroe Lab welcomes UC Davis undergraduates through **internships for course credit**. This is the first step for getting involved in research in our lab: every new undergraduate spends their first quarter with us as a for-credit intern. It gives you a real taste of laboratory research and gives both of us a chance to see whether the lab is a good fit before committing to more.

        **We are not currently able to offer paid undergraduate positions.** Students who complete an internship and do well may be considered for paid positions afterward, as funding allows.

        ---

        ## What you would do and learn

        Students in our lab spend most of their time doing molecular biology for plant genetics. That means a lot of hands-on chemistry: getting DNA out of plant tissue and preparing it for sequencing. Skills you can expect to develop include:

        - DNA extraction (including high molecular weight extractions for long-read sequencing)
        - Gel electrophoresis
        - Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)
        - Reagent preparation and basic lab chemistry
        - Plant growth, harvesting, and tissue preparation
        - DNA sequencing and next-generation sequencing library preparation
        - More depending on specific projects and your interests

        You will work closely with a graduate student or another lab member, attend lab meetings, and keep a proper lab notebook. If you are curious about graduate school, this is also a chance to interact with grad students and see what research life is actually like.

        ## What we look for

        Experience is not the important thing — many of our most successful students arrived with very limited experience in any of these techniques. What matters is **mindset and disposition**: genuine curiosity about learning new things, excitement about working in a real biology lab, reliability, and an interest in taking good notes and doing careful work.

        ## Where it can lead

        The internship is a starting point, not a dead end. Students who do well can go on to:

        - **Paid positions** in the lab, as funding allows
        - **Independent research projects** in later quarters
        - Experience and mentorship that strengthen graduate school or professional applications

        ---

        ## How internships for credit work at UC Davis

        You register for research credit under Grey as the instructor. Which course depends on your standing:

        - **PLS 199 (Special Study for Advanced Undergraduates)** — upper division students; 1-5 units
        - **PLS 192 (Internship)** — requires completion of 84 units; 1-12 units
        - **PLS 99 / PLS 92** — the equivalent options for lower division students

        All of these are graded Pass/No Pass and require instructor consent. **One unit corresponds to roughly three hours per week in the lab** over the quarter, so a typical 2-3 unit internship means about 6-9 hours per week. We generally ask for at least 6 hours per week so you can make real progress.

        Over one quarter, the goal is for you to become competent and reliable with the core techniques above, keep a complete lab notebook, and present what you did and learned at a lab meeting at the end of the quarter.

        Once we agree you are joining, we will coordinate the registration together with the department before the quarter's add deadline.

        ---

        ## Interested? Here is what to send

        Email Grey ([gmonroe@ucdavis.edu](mailto:gmonroe@ucdavis.edu)) **well before the start of the quarter you want to join — ideally a month or more** — so there is time to meet and set up registration. Please include:

        1. Your name, major, year, and expected graduation
        2. Which quarter you want to start
        3. How many hours per week you can realistically commit, and roughly which days (a copy of your class schedule helps)
        4. Relevant coursework you have taken (genetics, chemistry, biology labs)
        5. Any prior lab experience — it is completely fine if you have none
        6. A few sentences on why you are interested in this lab specifically and what you hope to learn
        7. A resume or CV if you have one (optional)

        We review inquiries each quarter and take a small number of students at a time, so telling us clearly when you want to start and what you can commit to goes a long way.
